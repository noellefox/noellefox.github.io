Project Datasets¶

To explore the relationship between MLB payroll spending and team success, we are using multiple datasets that capture both team-level and player-level information. Together these datasets will allow us to analyze how much each team spends on its roster, how that spending translates to on-field performance, and which specific player statistics teams appear to prioritize when allocating their funds. By combining these data sources, we will be able to uncover patterns that link financial investments to player performance and overall team outcomes.

Dataset 1: Team payrolls and performance This dataset contains each MLB team’s total payroll for a given season (from 2011 to 2024) along with their corresponding win-loss record. We chose this dataset because it will serve as the foundation for our analysis in that it allows us to test our initial question ‘Do teams that spend more money actually perform better?’

Question: ‘Does higher team payroll correlate with greater success, as measured by total wins/losses in a season?’

Statistics we can pull: average team payroll per season, top-performing teams per dollar spent, correlation coefficient between payroll and win percentage.

Link to data source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/christophertreasure/mlb-team-payrolls-2011-2024

It provides an understanding of how individual players contribute to team outcomes. We will use this dataset to explore what specific player characteristics or skills teams might be rewarding with higher salaries or greater investment. By linking this dataset with our third on player salaries, we can see what traits teams value more from a spending perspective.

Model Questions¶

While our exploratory analysis identified stolen bases, doubles, and walks as the three statistics where the most cost-efficient teams most exceeded league averages, these metrics alone do not fully explain league-wide efficiency. To fully address our project goal we ask these model questions which aim to identify the performance statistics that best predict Wins per Million and total wins. Now we will broaden the analysis to include all hitting metrics across all MLB teams.

  1. What player statistics best predict a team's wins per million dollars spent?
  • Independent Variable: Team level AB-weighted averages of hitting statistics
  • Dependent Variable: Wins per Million dollars spent
  • Method: a multiple linear regression model to estimate each stat's individual contribution so we can extract intepretable coefficiencts to explain which stats matter most
  1. Can we predict a team's total wins based on payroll and performance metrics?
  • Independent Variable: Payroll and Performance Stats
  • Dependent Variable: Total wins
  • Method: a multiple linear regression model to quantify how strongly each variable predicts wins and extract coefficients to show the direction and magnitude of each variables effect

Model One¶

We built a multiple linear regression model using all AB-weighted hitting statistics to predict Wins per Million dollars spent. This allows us to see which offensive metrics have the strongest impact on cost-efficient team performance.

In [109]:
# MODEL 1: Predict Wins per Million using ALL hitting stats

from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.metrics import r2_score, mean_squared_error

# Merge payroll with previously calculated team weighted stats
model1_df = payroll.merge(
    team_weighted_avgs.reset_index(),
    on='team code',
    how='inner'
)

# Dependent variable
y1 = model1_df['Wins per Million']

# Independent variables: ALL AB-weighted hitting stats
X1 = model1_df[stat_cols]     # stat_cols is the list of all hitting metrics

# Train-test split
X1_train, X1_test, y1_train, y1_test = train_test_split(
    X1, y1, test_size=0.2, random_state=42
)

# Fit model
model1 = LinearRegression()
model1.fit(X1_train, y1_train)

# Predictions
y1_pred = model1.predict(X1_test)

# Evaluation
print("Model 1 R^2:", r2_score(y1_test, y1_pred))
print("Model 1 RMSE:", np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(y1_test, y1_pred)))

# Coefficient table
coeff1 = pd.DataFrame({
    'Statistic': X1.columns,
    'Coefficient': model1.coef_
}).sort_values(by='Coefficient', ascending=False)

Conclusion¶

Our analysis uncovered that MLB team success is shaped not only by payroll size but also by how efficiently teams convert their financial resources into actual performance. Through EDA, we identified that small and mid-market teams can vary widely in their win totals despite similar spending, and that organizations like Tampa Bay (TB), Cleveland (CLE), and Oakland (OAK) consistently achieve the highest wins per million dollars spent. These teams outperform league-wide averages in key offensive areas—particularly stolen bases, doubles, and walks—suggesting that speed, extra-base hitting, and plate discipline help drive cost-effective success.

Our regression models reinforced and expanded these findings. Model 1 showed that hitting statistics can moderately predict a team's Wins per Million, explaining about half of the variation in efficiency. This indicates that a player's offensive skill does play a meaningful role in how well teams translate spending into performance, though it is not the sole contributor. Model 2, which incorporated both payroll and hitting metrics, captured a smaller share of the variation in total wins, emphasizing that while spending and offensive performance matter, team success also depends on factors beyond the scope of our datasets. Other factors may include pitching strength, defensive ability, injuries, and strategic management; these indicate a strong starting point for future research.

Together, these results highlight that high payrolls alone do not guarantee winning, and that strategic investment in the right kinds of offensive players can allow smaller-budget teams to compete at a high level. Ultimately, success in the MLB appears to arise from a combination of financial efficiency, roster construction, and underlying skill profiles, reinforcing the value of analytics in informing both spending decisions and long-term team strategy.
